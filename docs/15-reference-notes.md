# Reference notes — the visual target

> These four images are the feel we are aiming at. We are **not** copying any
> of them. We are extracting the shared DNA and building a different
> composition in 16:9 (see `25-art-pipeline.md` → Panel 01).
>
> Written out because the reviewers and future sessions cannot see the images
> themselves. If the originals are added to `docs/refs/`, this file still
> stands as the interpretation, and the images become the ground truth.

## The four references, described

### A — pale blue karst (portrait, the original brief)

Near-white misty range. Very sharp, thin karst spires, many overlapping, dense
mist sitting in every valley. Foreground: dark blue-grey rocks bottom-left, a
bare prunus branch with pink blossoms entering from the left, dark pines on the
rocks. A small pavilion on a crag right of centre. Still water at the bottom
with soft reflections. Palette is almost monochrome cool blue-grey with white
mist and **one pink accent**. Light is diffuse; no visible sun.

### B — warm dawn with waterfall (portrait)

Warm cream-peach sky at the top. Tall needle peaks. A white **waterfall**
dropping from the centre into mist. Heavy dark pine trees framing left and
right, entering the frame from the top corners. A terracotta pagoda with
strongly upturned eaves on a rocky crag, lower-left of centre. Layered mist
valleys. Foreground bottom-left: rocks, pines, and a **bright yellow-green
shrub** (the accent). Palette: warm cream sky → cool grey-teal rock → very dark
green pines → terracotta → one acid-green accent.

### C — ink wash (portrait)

Dramatic and near-monochrome. Almost **black vertical cliff faces** frame the
left and right edges. A tall pavilion silhouette sits dead centre on a rock
pinnacle above water. Thick **white mist swirls around the pavilion**, wrapping
it. Two tall, thin pines with flat layered needle pads enter from the top-left
and top-right. Bottom third: dark water, reflections, scattered rocks. Warm
pale-gold light behind the central mist. Palette: near-black ink, warm cream,
almost no hue elsewhere. **The most graphic of the four** — the one that proves
how much you can do with value alone.

### D — teal ranges with pagoda (portrait)

Lush and green. Many overlapping ridges with mist between every one. **Warm
gold light on the upper faces**, cool in the valleys. Dense dark-green
vegetation clinging to every ridge line. A large multi-roof pagoda/temple at
centre-bottom above a lake. A **red-orange maple accent** at left-centre. Dark
pines silhouetted in the right foreground. Water with reflections. Pale
blue-green sky, a couple of birds. Palette: teal-green + gold highlight + dark
green + red-orange accent.

## The shared DNA (this is the actual target)

Extracted from all four. A layer is on-target when it delivers these.

1. **Verticality and layering are the whole point.** Depth is conveyed
   *entirely* by stacked overlapping ridges separated by mist — never by a
   ground plane, never by perspective lines. Ridges are counted, not seen
   through.

2. **Atmospheric perspective is the depth cue.** Each successive ridge is paler,
   lower-contrast and cooler than the one in front. The far ridge is barely
   distinguishable from sky.

3. **Mist is volumetric, not a band.** It fills valleys, wraps around ridge
   bases, is dense in the low points of the composition, and thins toward ridge
   crests. It has a warm glow deep in it (B and C) — it is *lit*, not white
   paint.

4. **Warm light from above, cool in the valleys.** Ridge crests and left/upper
   faces catch a warm highlight; the faces falling away and the deep valleys are
   cool blue-teal. This warm-cool split is the single biggest thing that makes
   these images feel like paintings rather than diagrams.

5. **Vegetation clings to ridge lines.** Dark green clusters sit *on* the crests
   and shoulders — irregular, of varying size, deepening as they get nearer.
   Never evenly scattered dots.

6. **One tiny human structure is the focal point.** A pavilion or pagoda,
   roughly a third up from the bottom, at or near centre. It is small — its job
   is to give the mountains a scale, not to be the subject.

7. **Dark, heavy foreground framing at the edges.** Pines and rocks enter from
   the frame edges, usually top-corners or bottom-corners, cut off by the frame.
   This is what creates the sense of looking *into* a place.

8. **Exactly one warm accent, used once.** Pink blossoms (A), acid-green shrub
   (B), red maple (D). Never two, never in the middle of the composition.

9. **Water at the bottom, when present**, with soft vertical reflections that
   are darker and lower-contrast than the object reflected.

10. **Nobody is in it.** Not a single figure. The place is empty, which is what
    makes it somewhere you want to be. Our dragons will be the only living
    things, and they arrive rarely.

## What is deliberately different in ours

- **16:9, not portrait.** Verticality preserved by cropping massifs at the frame
  edge and opening a notch, rather than fitting peaks inside the frame.
- **The notch.** A near-empty, brightest-air corridor right of centre. It is
  the dragon corridor. None of the references have this, because none of them
  had to leave room for something to fly through.
- **Cool-dominant with one warm accent**, closer to A and C than to D. The
  wallpaper is on screen for hours; D's saturation would be exhausting.
- **Pixel art, not painting.** Detail is bought with value structure and
  silhouette, never with brush texture. No dithering-as-texture (see D-021).
